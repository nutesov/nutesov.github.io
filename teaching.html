How I teach

I want students to recognize asymmetric information and the principal–agent problem in any market or policy they meet, and to judge, with a model and with data, what each problem costs and how contracts and institutions can reduce that cost. Much of each class goes to applying the models: group exercises built on real cases and real data, in which students test what a model predicts against what actually happened. In some exercises students also use AI tools and then examine the mistakes the tools still make, so that they learn to check any result against the data and the model. Weekly problem sets, discussed at the start of the next class, show me where students need more help.
