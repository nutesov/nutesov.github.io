Ph.D. candidate in Agricultural and Applied Economics, University of Wisconsin–Madison.
On the 2026–27 academic job market.

I am an applied microeconomist working in political economy, economic history and development, with a second line of work in industrial organization.

My research is about problems of asymmetric information that arise in agriculture. Two of them run through my work: the taxation of farms, and the adoption of technology. I study them mostly in an autocratic environment, where the state taxes farms and allocates technology through delegated agents, with a widespread principal-agent problem.

Job market paper

Power Without Information: The Human Cost of Uniform Land Taxation in Subsistence Agriculture

A state that taxes farms does not see the harvest. The paper measures what a uniform land tax cost in excess deaths and expulsions, on the annual accounts of 272 Soviet collective farms of the Urals for 1933 and 1934, and divides the cost between the two constraints of the state that levied it: the information constraint, the state taxing a harvest it could not see, and the incentive constraint, the discretion over extraction that its agents obtained. The tax cost one member in twenty-eight over two harvests, 63 per cent of it through the delegation of extraction and 37 per cent through the information the state lacked.
